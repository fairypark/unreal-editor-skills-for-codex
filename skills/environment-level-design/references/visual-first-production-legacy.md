# Visual-First Production Legacy

Use this contract to make successful visual decisions survive the current map,
builder, and Codex task. Inherit the quality gates and cause-level lessons. Do
not blindly copy scene coordinates, instance counts, asset paths, light values,
or PCG parameters.

## Start every environment with visible intent

1. Inspect available assets before selecting the concept. Choose a direction
   in which the strongest asset families support one coherent place.
2. Produce two to four concept views that collectively cover arrival, hero,
   route, and reverse directions. Select one primary quality reference and
   record prohibited failure imagery.
3. Create the Visual Recipe and persistent `CameraActor` set before production
   layout. Any view used for a decision must remain available for recapture.
4. Prove a representative 10–30 m golden slice before broad dressing.
5. Let a separate read-only visual supervisor control stage transitions. Use
   `gpt-5.6-sol` with `xhigh` reasoning and the `visual_critic` role when
   supported. Every applicable category must score at least 4/5 and any hard
   failure forces `NO-GO`.

## Keep visual quality authoritative

- During concept matching and golden-slice work, visual quality outranks Actor
  count, instance count, collision completeness, PIE success, performance, and
  technical neatness.
- Do not impose a numerical instance limit. Reject visible tiling, equal
  spacing, repeated silhouettes, and uniform scatter in the fixed cameras.
- Default decorative foliage, water plants, small rocks, gravel, debris, and
  story props to `NoCollision`, no overlap events, and no Navigation influence.
  Retain only essential terrain and traversal collision.
- After visual approval, add the narrowest proxy collision without moving or
  deleting approved visual placement. Recapture the same cameras afterward.
- Select Landscape, mesh terrain, or a hybrid from the approved concept and
  golden-slice result. Landscape is a common tool, not a mandatory quality
  target. Prefer one non-World Partition Landscape when it is used unless the
  brief explicitly requires streaming.

## Apply proven image-level rules

### Ecology and water plants

- Treat empty terrain and uniformly sparse scatter as visual failures even
  when every point is grounded.
- Build ecology from overlapping canopy, understory, edge vegetation, ground
  cover, contact debris, density gradients, and intentional gaps.
- Build water plants as colonies with dense local overlap, irregular lobes,
  shoreline contact, and authored open-water corridors. Reject grids, uniform
  rings, repeated flower markers, and exposed repeated bases.
- Preserve negative space only when it improves focal hierarchy. A large
  unarticulated field is not automatically intentional.

### Atmosphere and lighting

- Allow a darker and softer result when it improves concept fidelity while
  retaining focal readability.
- Use Exponential Height Fog for world-scale distance separation and
  overlapping Local Fog Volumes for waterline mist. Evaluate mist from player
  height and an elevated audit camera.
- Solve a sharp empty horizon with both atmosphere and layered silhouettes.
  Fog cannot replace missing midground composition.
- Use a narrow local fill to recover a secondary story prop only when it does
  not compete with the primary focal subject. Revalidate the values per scene.

### Routes, stones, and PCG

- Make a route read as one width-bearing surface, not a centerline chain,
  cement-like strip, or combination of overlapping independent scatters.
- For gravel, use dense small rounded pieces, restrained larger accents,
  embedded contact, believable material variation, and irregular edges.
- Prefer one authored spline or closed route boundary projected to trusted
  terrain height and normal. Sample both the interior and edge before spawning.
- Split PCG responsibilities into subgraphs while keeping one orchestrator
  responsible for exclusions and final spawn. Require disjoint masks before
  combining multiple generators in the same visible zone.
- Treat sample projects as read-only pattern libraries. Rebuild the smallest
  project-owned benchmark that proves the needed rule.

### Landmarks and contact

- A landmark passes only when an uninformed reviewer can locate and identify it
  in the intended cameras. Require a readable silhouette, value separation,
  approach or shoreline integration, and believable support.
- A successful placement call or center trace does not prove contact. Validate
  the complete footprint of architecture, bridge ends, rocks, roots, trees,
  and multi-part assemblies.

## Preserve an independent repair loop

1. Capture clean fixed cameras with stable FOV, exposure, aspect ratio,
   scalability, and warmed temporal history.
2. Give concepts and evidence to the read-only supervisor without the
   builder's intended verdict or persuasive explanation.
3. If the gate fails, freeze the comparison cameras and accepted systems.
   Repair the weakest visible system and recapture the same views.
4. Change one major variable per failed iteration. Do not average away a score
   below 4, hide a failure with a new camera, or advance because the level
   saved successfully.
5. After three consecutive failures of the same macro composition, require a
   documented concept or composition reset instead of another density, fog,
   prop, or polish pass.

## Accelerate only approved quality

- Reuse independently approved modules by role: route surface, shoreline
  transition, ecological cluster, atmosphere stack, landmark contact, or
  narrative-prop lighting.
- Treat each reused module as a candidate in the new biome, scale, terrain,
  lighting, and camera context. Previous approval never transfers
  automatically.
- Automate setup, deterministic seeds, asset sets, persistent-camera capture,
  evidence receipts, and supervisor packet creation. Keep the aesthetic
  verdict outside builder automation.
- Measure time to the first valid concept package, first golden-slice capture,
  independent `GO`, `NO-GO` loop count, and fixed-camera recapture count.
- Never gain speed by skipping concept art, reducing camera coverage, lowering
  the 4/5 floor, removing independent review, or scaling a failed slice.
